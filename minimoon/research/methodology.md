# Grand Cayman research methodology

**Freshness:** 2026-08-18 (Pacific Time)  
**Stay normalized:** Monday, October 5 through Sunday, October 11, 2026; 6 nights; 1 room; 2 adults; USD.

## Pricing and confidence

`nightlyRange` is a practical **all-in planning range per room per night**, not a claim that every room or cancellation policy is available inside that band. `sixNightRange` is exactly the displayed nightly low and high multiplied by six. It excludes flights, airport transfers, meals, optional gratuities, and discretionary incidentals.

Confidence labels mean:

- **`exact-date`** — the hotel's direct booking engine returned inventory for Oct 5–11, 2026. Palm Heights met this bar: its SynXis results showed one-bedroom categories at $934–$1,074 per night before taxes and fees.[1] The displayed planning band adds the Cayman 13% tourist-accommodation tax, an observed 10% service-charge signal, and a reported $90 nightly resort fee, then rounds to a useful ten-dollar band rather than implying checkout-level precision.[10]
- **`adjacent-date`** — a live quote was captured for nearby dates or the same seasonal period, but not the target stay. No Cayman entry currently uses this label.
- **`generic-rate-signal`** — multiple public rate/fee signals establish a defensible planning range, but the exact target-date checkout could not be captured. Kimpton Seafire and The Ritz-Carlton use this label; their reported nightly resort fees are currently $95 and $110 respectively.[11][12] These figures should be repriced directly before any hold or booking.
- **`estimate`** — the weakest fallback, used only when no property-specific date or rate signal is available. No Cayman entry currently uses this label.

Ranges favor the room category recommended in `bestRoom` and nearby one-bedroom/king categories, not villas, multi-bedroom residences, club upgrades, packages, or points rates. Refundable rates can sit above the range. Taxes and mandatory fees can change, so the hard budget test must always use the booking engine's final payment summary.

## Weather treatment

Grand Cayman is not a weather-safe October beach choice. The Cayman Islands National Weather Service defines the wet season as mid-May through October, gives wet-season norms of 89.1°F daily maximum and 78.1°F minimum, and says average monthly rainfall peaks in September and October.[4] Tropical waves can bring a day or two of cloudiness, heavy showers, and thunderstorms; hurricane season also remains active. For that reason every Cayman hotel receives the same low weather score and **high** weather-risk label. Hotel quality does not diversify island-wide storm exposure.

## Route and travel friction

Miami to Grand Cayman is available nonstop, including on American's published route.[6] The asymmetry is the return: Google Flights reports **no nonstop GCM–SFO service** and a fastest connecting itinerary around 8 hours 39 minutes in its current route overview.[5] The site therefore describes the inbound as low-friction and the outbound as moderate-friction. MIA is the cleanest conceptual connection for an American itinerary, while DFW, ATL, or IAH may be practical on other schedules; exact Oct 11, 2026 flight numbers and legal connection times must be checked when ticketing.

## Scores and editorial judgment

The six 1–10 attributes are comparative editorial scores for this shortlist, not review averages:

- **romance** — atmosphere, couple appeal, and sense of occasion
- **privacy** — scale, seclusion, and how easily a couple can escape family/group traffic
- **service** — staffing depth, consistency signals, and concierge capability
- **foodSpa** — combined strength of dining and wellness
- **activities** — compelling on-property and easy nearby options
- **weather** — date-specific climate suitability, not annual climate

`role`, `summary`, and `honestCatch` synthesize the property's official amenity/room facts with its likely minimoon fit. Kimpton's official room inventory confirms private balconies and majority ocean views,[2] while Ritz-Carlton's official overview confirms its dining, renewed spa, golf, and broad recreation bench.[3] The catches deliberately penalize scale, family orientation, tower layout, social scene, or weather dependence rather than repeating brochure copy.

## Quotes

Each quote is short, verbatim, and linked to its named page. The quote is an outside editorial signal, not evidence for price. Condé Nast Traveler supplies all three hotel quotes so tone and source quality remain consistent across the Cayman set.[7][8][9]

## Imagery sourcing and attribution

Each hotel has exactly three local images: a place-setting/property frame, a room frame, and a food/spa/experience frame. Every image came from the hotel's official website, the hotel's official Cloudinary account, or Marriott's official media CDN. The original URL and source name remain beside each image in `cayman.json`; captions describe the scene, and alt text describes visible content rather than marketing claims.

Downloaded assets were converted to WebP, kept at their source aspect ratio, and verified by fully decoding the saved file. All are at least 1,336 pixels wide and below 900 KB. Local copies improve reliability and performance; attribution and source URLs remain mandatory even though the runtime no longer depends on the remote file.

## Recheck triggers

Re-run the rate check if the stay dates, guest count, room category, cancellation terms, or budget change; if a promotion appears; or before any booking decision. Recheck flights when airline schedules firm up and monitor the Cayman Islands National Weather Service and National Hurricane Center beginning a week before departure. Travel insurance should cover named-storm disruption and delay/cancellation terms appropriate to a two-coast itinerary.
